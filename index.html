The reason this pair is the right test
Parity says the currency of the high inflation country loses value by the difference in inflation.
For the euro against the dollar that difference is a point or two a year, buried under market noise. For Turkey it ran from 5 to 80 points a year. The signal is large enough to see, which is why this pair can actually test the idea rather than just fail to reject it.

What this means
Year by year changes track parity. The accumulated level does not.
By 2024 the lira sits roughly 43 percent below the level cumulative inflation alone would imply, and the real rate has swung from 41 percent overvalued to 65 percent undervalued along the way. This is the standard finding: parity is a long run anchor, not a level you can trade against in any given year.

10 — Part 2
Two competitors
Benchmark B, the efficient market forecast
rate in h days
= rate today
No parameters, nothing estimated.
Method S, random walk with drift
rate in h days
= today + h x drift
Drift is the average daily change in the first 250 days only.
Honest out of sample design
Estimate on days 1 to 250. Forecast days 251 to 510. The drift never sees the data it is scored on.
A third method, an autoregression on daily changes, is run as a cross check. Results are reported alongside.

Why the gap widens

Benchmark bias, 20 days
+0.0130
The mechanism
The benchmark is not just noisy, it is biased. It misses in the same direction almost every time.
Assuming no change means assuming the lira stops falling, and it falls on three days out of four. Over one day that costs little. Over sixty days the missed drift compounds while random error only grows with the square root of time, so the bias takes over completely. The drift method is nearly unbiased by construction: its average error over 20 days is 0.0027 against 0.0130 for the benchmark.

13 — Part 2
What this does not prove
What would be wrong to conclude
That currencies are forecastable in general.
That Meese and Rogoff were wrong.
That this method would make money.
What is actually true
The lira runs a managed, near one way crawl.
Its drift is large and stable, so it is estimable.
Run this on the euro and the benchmark wins.
And the two halves connect here
The drift that beats the random walk is the inflation differential from Part 1.
Drift over the full sample, 18.1 percent a year. Turkish inflation minus US inflation in 2024, 55.6 points. The daily crawl is the central bank letting parity happen on a schedule instead of all at once.
